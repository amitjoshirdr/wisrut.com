UNIT I

Introduction to marketing

Marketing = understanding what customers need, then satisfying those needs better than competitors, at a profit. Three key words: Customer · Value · Profit. It begins before production and continues after the sale.

Quick revision · Unit I
  • Need = basic requirement; Want = form shaped by culture; Demand = want + buying power.
  • 4Ps (McCarthy, 1960): Product, Price, Place, Promotion. Services add People, Process, Physical evidence (Booms & Bitner, 1981) = 7Ps.
  • Services: intangible, inseparable, variable, perishable — an empty hotel room tonight is lost income.
  • Marketing myopia (Levitt, 1960): loving the product more than the customer's need.
  • Micro environment: company, suppliers, intermediaries, customers, competitors, publics. Macro: demographic, economic, natural, technological, politico-legal, socio-cultural.
  • Environmental scanning feeds SWOT — Opportunities and Threats come from outside.

Quick revision · Unit II
  • Five stages: Need recognition → Information search → Evaluation → Purchase decision → Post-purchase behaviour.
  • Information sources: personal, commercial, public, experiential. Most information from commercial; most trust from personal.
  • Satisfaction = performance compared with expectation. Below → dissatisfied; equal → satisfied; above → delighted.
  • Cognitive dissonance (Festinger): post-purchase doubt; reduce with follow-up, warranty, service.
  • Perception: selective attention, distortion, retention. Learning: drive → cue → response → reinforcement.

Quick revision · Unit III
  • Bases (G-D-P-B): Geographic, Demographic, Psychographic, Behavioural.
  • Targeting (U-D-C-M): Undifferentiated, Differentiated, Concentrated (niche), Micromarketing.
  • Evaluate segments on size & growth, structural attractiveness, company objectives & resources.
  • Statement format: To (target) who (need), our (brand) is a (category) that (benefit).
  • Positioning bases: attributes, benefits, occasion, user, price-quality, against/away from competitors, product class.

Quick revision · Unit IV
  • Levels: Core (benefit) → Actual (features, brand, pack) → Augmented (warranty, delivery, helpline).
  • Consumer types: convenience (staple/impulse/emergency), shopping, speciality, unsought.
  • Brand name is spoken; brand mark is seen; a trademark is registered (Trade Marks Act, 1999).
  • Packaging: primary, secondary, shipping. Labels: brand, descriptive, grade (ISI, Agmark).
  • PLC strategies — Intro: awareness, skim or penetrate · Growth: share, preference · Maturity: modify market/product/mix · Decline: maintain, harvest, drop.
UNIT V

Pricing & distribution

Price is the only P that brings in revenue. Cost is the floor, the customer's perceived value is the ceiling. A sale happens when value ≥ price; profit when price > cost.

Quick revision · Unit V
  • Objectives: survival, current profit, market share, skimming, quality leadership.
  • Methods: cost-plus, break-even/target-profit, perceived-value, going-rate, sealed-bid.
  • Mark-up on sales: Price = Unit cost ÷ (1 − mark-up). ₹80 ÷ 0.80 = ₹100, not ₹96!
  • Channels: zero (direct), one, two, three-level. Merchant middlemen take title; agents do not.
  • Channel choice (M-P-C-M-C-E + cost): Market, Product, Company, Middlemen, Competitors, Environment.
  • Logistics: orders, warehousing, inventory, transport, handling, information — right goods, place, time, lowest total cost.
  • Promotion mix: advertising, sales promotion, personal selling, public relations. Push → dealers; Pull → consumers.

Model answer · Distinguish marketing and selling (5 marks)

Use a table with five headed rows: starting point (customer needs vs the factory's product), focus (satisfaction vs volume), means (integrated 4Ps vs selling and promotion), goal (profit through satisfaction vs profit through volume), time (before, during and after the sale vs mainly at the sale). Close with: "Selling is one tool of marketing; marketing is outside-in, selling is inside-out." Add Nisha asking tourists first.

Model answer · Break-even numerical (5 marks)

Fixed ₹1,20,000, VC ₹40, expected sales 4,000, mark-up 30% on sales.
(a) Unit cost = 40 + 1,20,000÷4,000 = ₹70. Price = 70÷0.70 = ₹100.
(b) Contribution = 100−40 = ₹60. BEP = 1,20,000÷60 = 2,000 units.
(c) For ₹60,000 profit: (1,20,000+60,000)÷60 = 3,000 units. Check: 3,000×60−1,20,000 = ₹60,000. Always show formula → substitution → unit.

Exam habits: open with a one-line definition (name Kotler, Armstrong, AMA); headings and numbered points; a table for "distinguish"; one Indian example per long answer; formula, substitution and unit in numericals; leave two minutes to check.
