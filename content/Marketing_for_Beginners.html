I

Introduction and Marketing Environment

Marketing means finding out what customers need, then creating, communicating and delivering value to them at a profit. It starts before the product is made and goes on after the sale.

Need → basic requirementWant → a specific wishDemand → want + ability to pay
Pahadi Pitara. Nisha fills 200 bottles and sells only 40. She had made first and thought later. After she asks tourists what they want, sales improve.

Five philosophies (each step is a later idea)

Marketing mix

4Ps (goods)

  • Product: what we sell
  • Price: what buyers pay
  • Place: where and how it reaches them
  • Promotion: how we tell them

3 more Ps (services)

  • People: staff and guides
  • Process: steps of the service
  • Physical evidence: rooms, signboard, uniforms

Total 7Ps for services.

Marketing environment

Micro (close to the firm)

  • The company itself
  • Suppliers
  • Marketing intermediaries
  • Customers
  • Competitors
  • Publics

Macro (wide forces)

  • Demographic
  • Economic
  • Natural
  • Technological
  • Politico-legal
  • Socio-cultural
Remember: the firm can influence the micro environment but can only adjust to the macro environment. A landslide that closes a road is a natural (macro) factor.
II

Consumer Behaviour

Consumer behaviour is the study of what people buy, why, when, where and how. A firm that understands it can serve buyers better and waste less money.

Pahadi Pitara. Mrs Sharma stops at the bus stop, asks about safety, checks Instagram, tastes a free sip and buys three bottles as gifts. Each step is a stage below.

Five stages of the buying decision

  1. Need recognitionBuyer feels a gap: thirsty, cold, curious.
  2. Information searchAsks friends, reads reviews, looks online.
  3. Evaluation of alternativesCompares brands on price, quality, trust.
  4. Purchase decisionChooses and pays.
  5. Post-purchase behaviourHappy or unhappy; repeats or complains; tells others.

Factors that affect buying

Cultural

Culture, sub-culture, social class, festivals and customs.

Social

Family, friends, reference groups, status and role.

Personal

Age, job, income, lifestyle, life stage.

Psychological

Motivation, perception, beliefs, attitudes, learning.

Remember: C-S-P-P. A belief like "hill products are pure" is psychological. A friend's suggestion is social.
III

Segmentation, Targeting and Positioning

No firm can serve everyone. So it segments the market, targets the best groups and positions its brand in their minds. This is called STP.

  1. SegmentationDivide the market into groups with similar needs.
  2. TargetingChoose which segments to serve.
  3. PositioningDecide how the brand should be seen by them.
Pahadi Pitara. Nisha finds three groups: tourists want a small bottle, families want a gift pack, shop owners want steady supply. She targets tourists and gift-buying families, and positions the brand as "pure Himalayan taste".

Bases of segmentation (G-D-P-B)

BasisDivides byExample

DemographicAge, gender, income, job, family sizeStudent packs

Requirements for effective segmentation (M-S-A-D-A)

MeasurableSubstantialAccessibleDifferentiableActionable
  • Measurable: we can find its size and buying power.
  • Substantial: big enough to be profitable.
  • Accessible: we can reach it through channels and media.
  • Differentiable: segments respond differently to different offers.
  • Actionable: we have the means to serve it.

Targeting and positioning

Targeting strategies

  • Undifferentiated: one offer for all
  • Differentiated: different offers for several segments
  • Concentrated: one segment only

Positioning by

  • Attribute or benefit
  • Use or occasion
  • User type
  • Competitor
  • Price and quality

IV

Product Decisions

A product is anything offered to satisfy a need: a good, a service or an idea.

Classification

Consumer goods

  • Convenience: bought often, little effort
  • Shopping: compared before buying
  • Specialty: special brand, buyer will search
  • Unsought: not planned, such as insurance

Industrial goods

Bought by firms to make other goods or run the business: raw materials, machines, supplies, services.

Product mix

Width = number of product lines. Length = total number of items. Average length per line = length ÷ width.

Example: tea has 4 items, biscuits 6, snacks 5. Width = 3, length = 15, average length = 5.

Brand, package, label, support

  • Branding: a name, sign or design that identifies a seller's goods. A registered brand is a trademark.
  • Packaging: protects the product and presents it.
  • Labelling: gives information such as contents, price, date and how to use.
  • Product support services: help before, during and after the sale, such as a helpline or free delivery.

V

Pricing and Distribution

Price is the amount a buyer pays for value received. It is the only P that brings money in.

Pricing objectives

  • Survival
  • Profit maximisation
  • Market share
  • Quality leadership

Factors affecting price

  • Cost
  • Demand
  • Competition
  • Government rules
  • Buyer's view of value

New-product pricing

Channels of distribution

  • Wholesaler: buys in bulk and sells in smaller lots to retailers.
  • Retailer: sells to the final consumer.
  • Logistics: transport, warehousing, inventory control and order processing.
  • Channel choice depends on: market, product, firm, middlemen, competitors and cost.
Pahadi Pitara. Nisha sets ₹100 a bottle after checking cost, value and rivals. She sells at her stall (direct), through tourist shops (one-level) and later a wholesaler (two-level).
