Chapter 1: Introduction to Marketing

What is Marketing?

Marketing is the business activity of understanding what customers need, and then satisfying those needs better than competitors, at a profit.

Three key words: Customer, Value, Profit.

Remember: Marketing starts before the product is made (by studying customers) and continues after the sale (through service and feedback).
Story: Nisha first made 200 bottles of Buransh squash because she liked the taste — only 40 sold. Then she asked tourists what they wanted. That shift — from "my product" to "customer's need" — is the shift from selling to marketing.

Marketing vs Selling

BasisMarketingSelling
Starts withCustomer needsThe factory / product
FocusSatisfactionSales volume
GoalProfit through customersProfit through sales
TimeBefore, during, after saleMainly at sale

The Five Marketing Philosophies

  1. Production: "Make more, make cheap." (Ford's Model T)
  2. Product: "Make the best." Danger: marketing myopia.
  3. Selling: "Push hard." Used for insurance, unsought goods.
  4. Marketing: "Serve the customer." (Jio's low data price)
  5. Societal: "Serve customer + society + profit." (ITC e-Choupal, Lifebuoy)

The Marketing Mix

4Ps for goods: Product, Price, Place, Promotion.

7Ps for services: add People, Process, Physical evidence.

Marketing Environment

Micro (close to firm, can influence): company, suppliers, intermediaries, customers, competitors, publics.

Macro (large forces, cannot control): demographic, economic, natural, technological, politico-legal, socio-cultural.

Chapter 2: Consumer Behaviour

What is Consumer Behaviour?

The study of the WHO, WHAT, WHY, WHEN, WHERE and HOW of buying. It covers what happens before, during, and after the purchase.

Consumer vs Customer: The consumer uses the product (the child eating biscuits). The customer buys it (the parent). A good marketer must please both.

The Five Stages of Buying Decision

  1. Need recognition — "I am thirsty" or "My phone is slow"
  2. Information search — asks friends, reads reviews, visits shops
  3. Evaluation of alternatives — compares brands on price, features, etc.
  4. Purchase decision — chooses brand, dealer, payment method
  5. Post-purchase behaviour — satisfied, dissatisfied, or delighted
Story: Mrs. Sharma feels thirsty (need), asks Hari Chacha if the squash is safe (search), tastes a sip (evaluation), buys three bottles (purchase), sends a photo and gets a free replacement for a loose cap (post-purchase).

Four Factors That Influence Buyers

  1. Cultural: culture, subculture, social class (widest influence)
  2. Social: reference groups, family, roles and status
  3. Personal: age, occupation, income, lifestyle, personality
  4. Psychological: motivation, perception, learning, beliefs and attitudes
Maslow's Hierarchy (from bottom): Physiological → Safety → Social → Esteem → Self-actualisation. A satisfied need no longer motivates.

Chapter 3: Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning (STP)

The Three Steps

  1. Segmentation: Divide the market into groups with similar needs.
  2. Targeting: Pick the group(s) you can serve best.
  3. Positioning: Place your brand clearly in the buyer's mind vs competitors.

Four Bases of Segmentation

BasisDivides byExample
GeographicWhere they liveWoollens in hills, cotton in plains
DemographicWho they areAge, income, gender, family size
PsychographicHow they think/liveLifestyle, personality, social class
BehaviouralHow they buyOccasion, benefits, loyalty, usage rate

Targeting Strategies

  • Undifferentiated: one offer for all (salt, sugar)
  • Differentiated: separate offers for several segments (Titan: Tanishq, Fastrack, Sonata)
  • Concentrated (niche): focus on one small segment (a boutique, one-exam coaching)
  • Micromarketing: tailor to individuals or local areas
Story: Nisha has limited money and a small kitchen. She chooses tourists and city gift-buyers (concentrated marketing) and positions Pahadi Pitara as "the hand-made, village-picked taste of Kumaon that you can carry home."

Positioning Statement Format

To (target segment) who (need), our (brand) is a (category) that (key benefit).

Chapter 4: Product Decisions

Three Levels of a Product

  1. Core product: the basic benefit (refreshment, communication, rest)
  2. Actual product: the physical item — features, brand, packaging
  3. Augmented product: extra services — warranty, delivery, helpline

Product Mix Dimensions

  • Width: number of product lines
  • Length: total items across all lines
  • Depth: versions of each item (sizes, flavours)
  • Consistency: how related the lines are

Branding, Packaging, Labelling

  • Brand: name, logo, slogan, trademark (legally protected)
  • Packaging: protects, contains, identifies, promotes, gives convenience
  • Labelling: informs — MRP, ingredients, FSSAI, "best before"

Product Life Cycle (PLC)

StageSalesProfitMain Aim

Chapter 5: Pricing and Distribution

Price Basics

Price is the only element of the marketing mix that brings in revenue. The other three (product, place, promotion) all cost money.

Rule: Cost is the floor. Customer's perceived value is the ceiling. The best price lies between them.

Two Pricing Strategies for New Products

SkimmingPenetration
Initial priceHighLow
AimHigh profit per unitHigh volume, large share
BuyersQuality-conscious, less price-sensitiveMass market, price-sensitive
ExampleNew smartphones, luxury goodsReliance Jio, new FMCG product

Channels of Distribution

  • Zero-level (direct): Producer → Consumer
  • One-level: Producer → Retailer → Consumer
  • Two-level: Producer → Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer
  • Three-level: Producer → Agent → Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer

Promotion Mix (Four Tools)

  1. Advertising: paid, non-personal (TV, hoardings, social media)
  2. Sales promotion: short-term incentives (discounts, coupons, BOGO)
  3. Personal selling: face-to-face (showroom staff, insurance agents)
  4. Public relations: favourable publicity, goodwill (press releases, CSR)

Important Questions for Exams

5-Mark Questions

  1. Define marketing. Distinguish between marketing and selling.
  2. Explain the five marketing philosophies with one example each.
  3. Explain the four characteristics of services (intangibility, inseparability, variability, perishability).
  4. What is consumer behaviour? Explain the five stages of the buying decision process.
  5. Explain Maslow's hierarchy of needs with a product example for each level.
  6. What is market segmentation? Explain any three bases with Indian examples.
  7. Distinguish between differentiated and concentrated marketing.
  8. What is a positioning statement? Write one for an imaginary brand.
  9. Explain the three levels of a product with an example.
  10. Explain the functions of packaging.
  11. Distinguish between skimming and penetration pricing.
  12. Explain the functions of channels of distribution.
  13. Explain the four elements of the promotion mix.

10-Mark Questions

  1. Explain the marketing environment — micro and macro factors — with Indian examples.
  2. Explain the factors influencing consumer buying decisions (cultural, social, personal, psychological).
  3. What is STP? Explain segmentation bases, targeting strategies and positioning with examples.
  4. Explain the product life cycle and the marketing strategies at each stage.
  5. Explain the factors affecting the price of a product (internal and external).
  6. What is a channel of distribution? Explain its types and functions.

Quick Quiz — Tap to Reveal Answers

Try each question first. Then tap "Show answer" to check.

Q1. Marketing begins — (a) after sale, (b) before production and continues after sale, (c) only at advertising, (d) only in the shop.

Answer: (b). Modern marketing starts with studying customer needs before production and continues with after-sales service.

Q2. A want backed by the ability to pay is called — (a) need, (b) utility, (c) demand, (d) satisfaction.

Answer: (c) demand. Need → Want → Demand (when buying power is added).

Q3. The belief that customers will not buy enough unless pushed belongs to — (a) production concept, (b) selling concept, (c) product concept, (d) societal concept.

Answer: (b) selling concept. It relies on heavy selling and promotion, common for unsought goods like insurance.

Q4. Which philosophy balances company profit, customer wants AND society's well-being?

Answer: Societal marketing concept. It serves the "three Ps" — People, Planet, Profit.

Q5. The 4Ps of the marketing mix are — ?

Answer: Product, Price, Place, Promotion. Given by E. Jerome McCarthy (1960).

Q6. An empty hotel room tonight cannot be sold tomorrow. This shows which characteristic of services?

Answer: (c) Perishability. Services cannot be stored.

Q7. The first stage of the consumer buying decision process is — ?

Answer: (a) Need recognition. Memory trick: N-I-E-P-P — Need, Information, Evaluation, Purchase, Post-purchase.

Q8. Doubt felt after a costly purchase ("Did I choose right?") is called — ?

Answer: (b) Cognitive dissonance. Marketers reduce it with thank-you messages, warranty, and good after-sales service.

Q9. Culture, subculture and social class are — (a) personal factors, (b) cultural factors, (c) psychological factors, (d) social factors.

Answer: (b) cultural factors. They have the widest and deepest influence on buying.

Q10. In Maslow's hierarchy, the highest-level need is — ?

Answer: (b) Self-actualisation. Order from bottom: Physiological → Safety → Social → Esteem → Self-actualisation.

Q11. STP stands for — ?

Answer: Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning. The customer-driven marketing strategy.

Q12. Selling woollens in hills and coolers in plains is which type of segmentation?

Answer: (d) Geographic segmentation. It divides by location and climate.

Q13. Positioning refers to the place a product occupies — (a) on the shelf, (b) in the buyer's mind vs competitors, (c) in the warehouse, (d) in the price list.

Answer: (b) in the buyer's mind relative to competitors. Positioning is about perception, not the physical product.

Q14. The basic benefit the buyer is really buying is the — (a) augmented product, (b) core product, (c) actual product.

Answer: (b) core product. Example: for a phone, the core benefit is "staying connected".

Q15. The number of different product lines a company carries is the _____ of its product mix.

Answer: Width. Memory trick: W-L-D-C — Width (lines), Length (items), Depth (versions), Consistency.

Q16. Sales are low, profits are negative, heavy promotion is needed. Which PLC stage?

Answer: (b) Introduction. The product is new and little known.

Q17. The only element of the marketing mix that brings in revenue is — ?

Answer: (c) Price. Product, place and promotion all involve costs.

Q18. Setting a high initial price for a new product to earn maximum revenue from early buyers is — ?

Answer: (b) Market-skimming pricing. "Skim the cream" — high price, fewer buyers, high margin.

Q19. A channel with no intermediary is called — ?

Answer: (d) Zero-level (direct) channel. Producer → Consumer directly.

Q20. A push strategy is mainly directed at — (a) consumers, (b) competitors, (c) channel members, (d) government.

Answer: (c) channel members. Push = promote to dealers. Pull = promote to final consumers.

Final Tip for the Exam

Always:
  • Start with a one-line definition (name the author if you remember — Kotler, Armstrong).
  • Use headings and numbered points.
  • Add one Indian example per long answer (Amul, Jio, Tata Salt, Pahadi Pitara).
  • Draw a simple labelled diagram where it fits.
  • Use a table for "distinguish between" questions.
