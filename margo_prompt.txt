# Prompt Template: High-Trust News Article Generator

Fill in the two bracketed fields before sending. Everything else stays as-is.

## THE PROMPT

You are writing a news article for students in **[FIELD — e.g., "renewable energy," "neuroscience," "constitutional law"]**. The target length is **[LENGTH — e.g., "500-700 words," "a short 250-word briefing"]**.

This article must be trustworthy enough that a student could cite it in coursework. Follow this process exactly, in order. Do not skip to writing before completing the research steps.

### Step 1 — Research before writing
Search the web for the most recent, significant developments in this field. Do not rely on your training knowledge for any factual claim, date, statistic, name, or quote — your training data has a cutoff and may be outdated or wrong. Run enough searches to:
- Identify a genuinely current story (not something already resolved, superseded, or stale)
- Confirm the story's timeline from at least two independent sources where possible
- Check whether the situation has continued to evolve since the most recent article you find (search again, more recently, if needed)

### Step 2 — Verify before committing to claims
For every specific fact (a date, a number, a quote, a named official, an outcome), confirm it appears in a source you actually retrieved — never fill in a plausible-sounding detail from memory. If sources disagree, say so in the article rather than picking one silently. If you cannot verify something, leave it out rather than guessing.

### Step 3 — Write the article
Structure:
- **Headline**: factual, not clickbait
- **Dateline**: state the date you are writing this (today's date) and the date(s) of the key events covered
- **Body**: lead with the most recent, most important development; explain background only as needed for a student audience; attribute every claim to its source inline (e.g., "according to [Source], ...")
- **Why it matters**: 2-3 sentences connecting the news to what a student in this field should understand or watch next

### Step 4 — Self-check before finalizing
Before presenting the article, explicitly answer these to yourself and adjust the article if any fail:
- Is every factual claim traceable to a specific source I retrieved this session?
- Is the most recent event in this article actually recent relative to today's date — or am I describing something already superseded?
- Have I avoided vague attributions like "sources say" or "reports indicate" without naming the source?
- Would a knowledgeable person in this field find anything here outdated, speculative-but-stated-as-fact, or unverifiable?

### Step 5 — Disclose sourcing and limits
At the end of the article, include a short **"Sources & Verification"** note listing:
- The sources actually used (name/outlet, date of publication)
- The date this article was generated
- Any aspect of the story that was still developing or unconfirmed at time of writing

If, after searching, you cannot find genuinely recent, verifiable news in this field, say so directly instead of producing a plausible-sounding article anyway.

## Notes for use
- Swap in **[FIELD]** and **[LENGTH]** each time.
- If the AI you're using doesn't have live web search, this prompt will still improve structure, but the recency/accuracy guarantees won't hold — tell the AI explicitly if search isn't available so it doesn't claim verification it can't do.
